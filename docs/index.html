Portfolio project · technical report

Third-party reference data hub

A small, working hub on AWS that ingests public registries (GLEIF, SEC EDGAR, OpenFIGI), resolves them into one golden copy of companies and their identifiers, groups companies by their ultimate parent, and answers “may this application use this dataset for this purpose?” from machine-readable licenses.

Built at small scale with real public data. Every figure on this page was measured on the deployed pipeline (Athena, AWS CLI) and carries its date. Source code, ADRs and runbooks: github.com/LuisRodriguezzz/third-party-reference-data-hub (most documentation is in Spanish).

1The problem

A bank knows the same counterparty under different codes depending on the desk: the LEI in lending, the ISIN of each bond in investments, a ticker or FIGI in trading, the CIK in credit research on US issuers. To answer “if this group defaults, how much do we lose?” someone has to say that all those codes are the same company, and which companies belong to the same group.

The LEI was created for exactly this, but on its own it is not enough:

  • It identifies legal entities, not securities. The ISIN↔LEI link is a separate file.
  • Not every source reports it. The SEC does not publish the LEI of its registrants: the field came back empty for 50 of 50 companies sampled, so CIK↔LEI has to be inferred.
  • Registry data has its own quality issues. In the GLEIF full file of 2026-10-10, 34.9 % of the records were LAPSED, and records can go backwards in time (postmortem 0001).

On top of that, external data arrives with licenses: some sources can be used for anything, some ask to be cited, a market data feed typically forbids redistribution and non-display use. A bank has to be able to show that each use of a dataset, including data derived from it, is within what its license allows.

2What the hub does

It lands four public sources untouched in an immutable zone, validates and deduplicates them into typed Iceberg tables (rows that break the contract go to quarantine with the rule that failed), and builds with dbt a golden copy: one row per SEC company with its LEI, the path that found it and explicit conflict / ambiguous flags, plus a cross-reference of CIK, LEI, ISIN, FIGI and ticker. It groups companies by ultimate parent, and a private API answers license questions from DCAT and ODRL policies. One Step Functions pipeline runs it all and watches itself with metrics and alarms.

4Architecture

One S3 bucket with a prefix per layer, Iceberg tables in the Glue Data Catalog, Athena for SQL. Lambda downloads, Glue (Spark) writes bronze and silver, dbt on Athena builds gold inside CodeBuild. No servers running between runs, no VPC.

5How the matching is measured

The golden copy goes codes first (ticker → OpenFIGI → ISIN → LEI) and uses the normalized legal name only as a fallback, restricted to US LEIs or LEIs that issued securities. Ties are broken by “issued securities”, then ISSUED, then US; if a tie remains, no LEI is assigned, because a false link adds exposure to the wrong company, which is worse than a missing one.

What the 12 errors teach

  • The only wrong link picked the subsidiary that issues the bonds: in a holding structure the debt is often issued by a subsidiary, and the tie-break prefers the entity that issued securities.
  • 7 misses are a deliberate trade-off: foreign issuers quoted in the US whose LEI is not American and has no securities in the ISIN↔LEI file. The rule that drops them is the one that stops a same-name company abroad from winning. High precision, paid for in recall; the next step is to accept a foreign LEI when its jurisdiction matches the SEC’s place of incorporation.
  • The rest: one GLEIF legal name in Japanese script (transliterated names are not loaded yet), one company renamed and redomiciled, one no longer in the SEC list it was drawn from.

6Licenses and the permissions API

The catalog is in DCAT and each license in ODRL. Prohibitions override permissions, no explicit permission means no, purposes follow a hierarchy, and a derived dataset inherits the restrictions and duties of every source upstream. CI evaluates all application × dataset × purpose combinations (432 today) into a decision table published with each version; the Lambda only looks up a row, so every answer says which policy version produced it and can be reproduced later. Each decision is logged as one JSON line with the signing IAM principal.

The endpoint is a private Lambda Function URL with AuthType = AWS_IAM: every request is SigV4-signed, an unsigned one gets 403, and an unknown name is a 400 that says which one, never a silent “no”. A real example from the README: the golden copy may be used for credit risk, with the duty to cite the SEC, inherited through lineage because it is built from SEC data.

{"app": "riesgo_corporativo", "dataset": "gold_empresas", "proposito": "riesgo_credito"}

7Exposure by economic group

gold.grupos maps every LEI to its ultimate parent as published by GLEIF (a join, no recursion): 3,460,622 LEIs, 133,250 of them with an ultimate parent. Only accounting consolidation forms a group; fund management, sub-funds, feeders and branches do not.

The loan portfolio is synthetic and declared as such: real LEIs with invented amounts, products and loans (296 loans to 281 LEIs, generated with a fixed seed). It exists so the question makes sense. The exposure figures it produces are not findings and are not reported here; they only show that the aggregation works.

8Local consumers: counterparty search and risk aggregation

In a bank the golden copy is consumed by other systems. Two of them run locally, in Podman, from official images with a pinned version (opensearchproject/opensearch:3.9.0 and container-registry.oracle.com/database/free:23.26.3.0-lite). They receive a copy exported from Athena with read-only queries and write nothing back. They are not part of the hub. Managed OpenSearch was ruled out on cost and risk: it bills by the hour while it is on and could spend the account’s credit in days, and even the serverless option has a minimum capacity billed when idle. Locally they cost nothing and start and stop with one command (ADR 0014).

OpenSearch as a counterparty search engine

OpenSearch is the fork of Elasticsearch 7.10 maintained by the Linux Foundation under the Apache 2.0 license; the Query DSL is practically the same as Elasticsearch’s. The index holds one document per company keyed by LEI (by CIK when there is no LEI): the whole golden copy (8,029 CIKs) plus the US companies in GLEIF with an ISSUED or LAPSED record (342,373), for 346,831 documents, 83.5 MB of index. The mapping is strict, names go through an analyzer that drops case, accents and legal forms, and every code is also copied into one identificadores keyword field.

Real results (2026-10-22, on the gold snapshot of 2026-10-21):

  • “berkshire hathway” (one letter missing), with fuzziness: AUTO: the listed parent BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY INC. comes first, followed by its US subsidiaries from GLEIF, which is what an analyst booking a loan needs.
  • “apple computer” (Apple’s name until 2007): Apple Inc. comes first, but fuzzy search also finds “Compugen” and “Compute” two letters away from “computer”. That is why the golden copy match does not use fuzzy matching: here a person looks and chooses; in the match, a false positive would add exposure to the wrong company.
  • Exact lookup by any identifier: one terms query with an ISIN (US5949181045), a CIK (0001067983), an LEI and a lowercase ticker (aapl) returns four companies, among them MICROSOFT CORPORATION for the ISIN, BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY INC. for the CIK and Apple Inc. for the ticker.

Oracle Free as a consuming risk system

Oracle AI Database 26ai Free plays the risk system that sums exposure by economic group by walking the direct consolidation tree, instead of using the published ultimate parent as gold does. CONNECT BY (Oracle’s own syntax) and recursive WITH (standard SQL, with SEARCH DEPTH FIRST and CYCLE) return exactly the same 8 rows, in the same order for the same tree. A GROUP BY ROLLUP (grupo, empresa) gives each loan, each group subtotal and the total in one query; for the five most exposed multi-company groups, both approaches agree, and they match gold.

Putting both heads side by side, loan by loan: 276 of 296 loans (93 %) land in the same group; 20 do not. Of those 20, 16 belong to a company that declares no direct parent, so the tree has nowhere to climb, and 4 to a chain that stops before the published head; there are 0 cases where GLEIF publishes no ultimate parent. All 20 go the same way: the tree splits groups, never merges two, and a split group hides exposure, the expensive error in credit. Amounts are not shown because the portfolio is synthetic.

9Operation

  • Every change by pull request. CI posts terraform plan on the PR; on merge to main it builds the package once, publishes it to S3 under an immutable version and runs terraform apply. Nobody applies from a laptop.
  • OIDC, no stored keys. GitHub authenticates to AWS with OIDC: a read-only role for PRs and a separate apply role for main.
  • Costs. Building everything so far moved the account’s credit balance from 177.44 USD (2026-09-27, before any hub resource) to 176.78 USD (2026-10-21). A full run cost about 0.21 USD; a run with more new data cost 0.32 USD of Glue the same day. Guardrails: a 10 USD monthly budget, an Athena workgroup that cancels any query reading more than 1 GB, 14-day log retention, no NAT or VPC endpoints. Details in docs/costos.md.
  • The daily schedule is off, on purpose. It exists in Terraform as DISABLED; a daily run would cost an estimated 6 to 10 USD a month against the 10 USD budget, so the pipeline is run by hand.
  • Observability. A data quality scorecard, six CloudWatch metrics, email alarms (including a failed execution), and Iceberg snapshot cleanup, all in the same pipeline.
  • Tests on business rules only: uv run pytest runs 143 of them locally, no AWS needed; dbt unit tests cover each matching rule with invented rows.

Postmortems (blameless) and runbooks

  • Postmortem 0001: silver kept a version GLEIF no longer published, because “newest last_update wins” can go backwards. The rule became “the latest published file wins”.
  • Postmortem 0002: the documented reprocess did not recompute anything; it was replaced by an explicit full refresh mode.
  • Infrastructure runbook: from an empty AWS account to a running pipeline, plus operation and teardown. Also dbt and local consumers.

11Known limitations

  • Foreign issuers. Roughly a fifth of SEC registrants are foreign; their US ticker is often an ADR, and their GLEIF name may be in another language or script. Many end without an LEI.
  • Companies without an LEI look the same as companies whose LEI was not found.
  • Exact name matching only. No fuzzy matching until the gold set shows it is needed.
  • Direct parent without an ultimate parent: 7,483 companies declare only a direct parent and are treated as the head of their own group. Ownership percentages are not in GLEIF level 2, so a group is all or nothing.
  • One environment (dev). The code is ready for more, but promotion between environments has not been exercised.
  • Decision logs are kept 14 days, like every log in the hub. A bank would keep them for years.
  • The calling application is self-declared in the permissions API: the IAM identity is verified and logged, but not yet bound to an application.
  • Scale. Small on purpose, and the daily schedule is off; nothing here has run in production.

Resumen en español

Un hub de datos de referencia de terceros, chico y funcionando en AWS, con datos públicos reales. Baja GLEIF, la relación ISIN↔LEI, las relaciones de control de GLEIF, la SEC y OpenFIGI; los guarda tal cual en landing, los valida y deduplica en tablas Iceberg (con cuarentena) y arma con dbt la golden copy: de 8.029 empresas de la SEC, 4.245 (52,9 %) quedaron con LEI, primero por códigos publicados y después por nombre, sin adivinar cuando hay empate. Contra un gold set de 100 empresas al azar, etiquetado a ciegas (una persona auditó 10: coincidieron los 10), el cruce tiene 98,4 % de precisión (IC 95 %: 91,5 % a 99,7 %) y 84,9 % de recall (IC 95 %: 75,0 % a 91,4 %). Agrupa por controlante final y suma una cartera sintética (sus cifras no son hallazgos). Las licencias están en DCAT y ODRL y una API privada responde si una aplicación puede usar un dataset para un propósito. Todo corre en un pipeline de Step Functions (medido el 21-oct: 31 min 38 s, unos 0,21 USD), con Terraform aplicado por PR. Dos consumidores locales en Podman, OpenSearch y Oracle Free, muestran cómo se usa la golden copy. La documentación completa, en español, está en el repositorio.
